How it works

One Mac at the front of the room. The person speaking does nothing differently.

1

Speak

A microphone on the speaker. The app streams the audio to Tencent 實時語音翻譯 and gets each sentence back, translated, as it finishes.

2

Show

A Display window on the projector, a transparent Overlay on the slides, or the share link and QR code for phones. Presets for a portrait strip, a landscape bar, or text over slides.

3

Keep

Recording never depends on the network. Afterwards: a complete set of subtitles from the cloud, an MP4 with the text burned in, and a learning summary as a PDF.

Where it helps

Any situation where following the words is the difference between attending and taking part.

Conferences and talks會議・講座

The venue screen, the slides, and every phone in the audience, from one laptop.

Classes and workshops課堂・工作坊

Students follow on their own screens; the recording and summary become the notes.

Meetings會議室

A meeting room with a mix of Cantonese and Mandarin speakers reads the same subtitle line.

Video and audio files影片・錄音

Drop a file in the web app: subtitles in minutes, edit the cues, export SRT, VTT, text or MP4.

Made for the room

Every part of the app was shaped by running it at real talks.

1Overlay on any displayTransparent window over the slides, placeable on every screen, including virtual ones, from the 字幕 menu bar item.
2Phones follow alongA share link and QR code; attendees choose translation, original or both and their own text size. Audio never leaves the Mac.
3Recording that cannot dropMP3 and subtitle files are written locally while the talk runs; a lost connection pauses subtitles, not the recording.
4Re-subtitle via cloudAfter the talk, the whole recording is recognised and translated in one pass, filling any holes a connection drop left.
5GlossaryNames, places and terms the recogniser should favour, shared with your team.
6Learning summaryA concise summary with clickable timestamps, as Markdown or an A4 PDF.

Files, too

Upload a video or audio file up to five hours. Choose the spoken language and the subtitle language; the cues arrive split at punctuation, at most 22 characters a line, with the timing of the speech.
